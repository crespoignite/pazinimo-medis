## From a problem you notice to an idea you present with confidence

A three-day, hands-on Business Academy for pupils in grades 7 to 9 who want to create, collaborate and get close to the world of business. Over three intensive days pupils form teams, look for real problems, build solutions, prepare a first prototype and present their idea to an invited panel. Here entrepreneurship is learned through experience, not from textbooks: by observing the surroundings, talking with people from the business world, testing their own ideas and learning from the feedback they receive.

- For pupils in grades 7 to 9
- 3 days
- Every day 9:00 to 17:00
- Only 20 participants
- 4 teams of 5 pupils

## What is the Business Academy?

Entrepreneurship is more than the ability to start a business. It is the skill of noticing a problem, understanding a person's need, offering a valuable solution and presenting your idea convincingly. In the Business Academy pupils travel the whole path of an idea in three days:

notice a problem → choose a direction → build a solution → prepare a prototype → present the idea

Participants do not need a business idea or any special knowledge in advance. What matters most is curiosity, a wish to try things and readiness to work in a team.

## What will we do?

### Day 1: Discover

On the first day participants get to know each other, form teams and work out what strengths each person can bring to the shared work. In a city challenge, pupils observe their surroundings, people's daily lives and the services on offer. They learn to notice inconveniences, unsolved problems and needs that can become the start of a new idea. Teams build a bank of real problems and possible ideas, discuss their observations and select the most interesting directions. The programme also includes a visit to ROCKIT, where participants get a closer look at the start-up world and hear how a real problem can grow into a business idea.

Result of the day: a formed team and a bank of real problems and ideas, from which one direction will be chosen for further work.

### Day 2: Build

On the second day each team chooses one problem or business idea to work on. Using the design thinking method, pupils work out:

- who the chosen problem matters to;
- what people's real needs are;
- which solutions could be offered;
- how the different solutions are strong or weak;
- how to turn an abstract idea into a concrete prototype.

Teams create several possible solutions, weigh the alternatives, choose the strongest direction and prepare a first prototype of their product or service. The programme includes a hands-on session with FIRSTPICK representatives from the business and investment world. Pupils will better understand what investors pay attention to, how new ideas are assessed and what an idea needs in order to grow.

Result of the day: a chosen problem, a clearly defined user, a solution idea, a first prototype and expert insights.

### Day 3: Present

On the third day teams learn to present their project clearly and convincingly. Participants meet the structure of a strong idea presentation:

problem → who it matters to → proposed solution → how it works → why it is valuable

With roles divided, pupils prepare presentations, rehearse and learn to answer questions. In practical presentation training, teams present their first version to Junior Achievement mentors, receive feedback and use it to improve both the prototype and the story of their idea. The academy closes with a final presentation to an invited panel. Panel members ask questions, share observations and give participants valuable feedback.

Result of the day: each team prepares and presents its project or business idea and prototype to the panel.

## What will participants gain?

In the Business Academy pupils build skills that help not only in starting a business but also at school, in everyday situations and in planning their future.

Participants will learn to:

- notice real problems and opportunities;
- understand other people's needs better;
- think creatively and critically;
- generate and assess different ideas;
- make decisions and justify their choices;
- collaborate and share roles;
- build a first prototype of a product or service;
- present their thoughts clearly, boldly and convincingly;
- take feedback and use it to improve an idea;
- understand better how the world of business, start-ups and investment works.

Most of all, participants get a safe space to try things, make mistakes, change direction and see how their own thoughts can step by step become a real solution.
